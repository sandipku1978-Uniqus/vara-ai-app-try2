The language pass

Two editors read the paper against one brief: polished and advisory, still direct where the paper takes a view, with no change to any fact, figure, quotation or source. The house rules still hold: at most three sentences a paragraph, exact figures, no firm names, US spelling.

  • Plain terms for insider shorthand"The file" becomes the comment letters or the comment record. "The release" becomes the SEC's proposal or the proposing release. "The line" becomes the threshold, and "the opinion" the auditor's attestation.
  • Advice rather than orders"Fix your status under both rule sets" becomes "Confirm filer status under the current and proposed rules". The eight decisions become numbered recommendations.
  • Fewer stylistic ticsFragments, stacked short sentences, colon reveals, "not X but Y" endings, rhetorical questions and quips in table cells are recast as full sentences.
  • The proposal is still a proposal"The line rises" becomes "would rise", and estimates are attributed to the SEC or to commenters.
  • The force stays where it countsThe thesis sentence, "should prepare" in the Point of View, and the closing lines of §4 to §6 stay firm. Ten places are flagged where a softer version was rejected.
  • Length heldThe front half is 3.0% longer and the back half 5.7%. Merging the decision boxes absorbs most of the growth on page 19. If a page overflows, a listed set of optional edits comes out first.

Your two-sentence version ("Much of the public comment focuses on where the threshold should be set. For finance leaders, the more pressing question is a different one.") would give this paragraph four sentences, against the house limit of three. The semicolon keeps it at three. If you prefer your version, the paragraph's last sentence can move to the next paragraph.
