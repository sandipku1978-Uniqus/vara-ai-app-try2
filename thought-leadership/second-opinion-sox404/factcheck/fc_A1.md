## Material omissions (release facts a CFO or audit committee chair would expect)

1. **Broader disclosure scaling for all NAFs** (Rel. p. 56). Beyond SRC relief, NAFs could omit:
   - risk factors in Forms 10-K and 10-Q;
   - Item 305 market-risk disclosure;
   - CD&A;
   - the performance graph;
   - Item 302(a) supplementary data.

   They could also use Article 8 of Regulation S-X (two years of financial statements, no Article 5, 7 or 9 formats; Rel. pp. 49, 86). Pay ratio and pay versus performance would go (Rel. pp. 67-68, 71). The paper reduces this to "scaled disclosure".
2. **Scale.** 4,825 registrants would be NAFs, which is 80.7% of registrants but 6.5% of float (Rel. pp. 45, 127). The 1,596 newly exempt are 60% of all registrants that obtain an attestation today (Rel. p. 130).
3. **The auditor still looks at controls.** NAFs keep the 404(a) assessment. The audit still requires an understanding of ICFR under AS 2110 and may test controls under AS 2301 (Rel. p. 51). Critical audit matters still apply to non-EGC NAFs (Rel. p. 70 n. 231).
4. **SEC's cost evidence for the "price the saving" step.** The SEC's 2019 estimate was $210,000 per issuer per year. GAO (June 2025) found a median increase of $219,000 on transition. The SEC expects costs for the newly exempt to be higher because audit fees rise with size, and warns that fees may not fall symmetrically on exit (Rel. pp. 131-132, n. 350). The SEC estimates a total aggregate annual monetized benefit of $1,874,534,145 (Rel. p. 167).
5. **Transition mechanics** (Rel. pp. 97-99):
   - each registrant assesses status as of the fiscal year-end before the rules take effect, at any time up to the day before the next fiscal year-end;
   - one that does not assess is deemed to keep LAF status;
   - accommodations start with the next filing after the assessment;
   - a former LAF may voluntarily keep LAF reporting (Rel. p. 33 n. 132).
6. **Seasoning effect.** 280 current LAFs (13.2%) have under 60 months of reporting (Rel. p. 113 n. 304). Seasoning alone moves the LAF count from 1,234 to 1,146 (Rel. p. 177).
7. **Possible disclosure of voluntary attestations.** RFC 21 asks whether the results of a voluntarily obtained attestation should have to be disclosed (Rel. p. 84). This bears directly on the "keep, drop or replace" decision.
8. **Shareholder and governance effects.** Loss of say-on-pay, frequency and golden-parachute votes (proposed Rule 14a-21(d)). The Rule 10C-1 compensation-committee listing-standard exemption extends to NAFs (Rel. p. 87).
9. **Banks.** The FDIC Part 363 attestation applies at $5 billion or more of institution assets. 87 of 153 banking LAFs would become NAFs (EA Table 8, Rel. p. 162). RFC 13 asks whether banks should keep Item 305 market-risk disclosure (Rel. p. 82).
10. **Interaction with the semiannual proposal.** Semiannual filers would measure float over the last 10 trading days of the first semiannual period (Rel. p. 30 n. 118). SNFs would get five extra days for semiannual reports (Rel. p. 87 n. 242).
11. **Small entity definitions.** The release also updates the Regulatory Flexibility Act small-entity definitions (Rules 157 and 0-10, last set in 1986; Rel. pp. 99-100).
