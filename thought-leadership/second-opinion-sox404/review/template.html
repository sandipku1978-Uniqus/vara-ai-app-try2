Corrections that change the argument

Contradicted by source

The 2008–09 insider survey

The draft says respondents judged 404(b)'s benefits to outweigh its costs. That is the release's paraphrase. The study behind it (Alexander et al., 2013) found that on average they did not, though net benefits were higher where an auditor attested and rose with experience.

respondents judged the benefits of Section 404(b) compliance to outweigh the costs
The study behind the survey is less favourable: on average respondents did not judge the benefits to outweigh the costs…
§1.2 · Register D-23
Incorrect

What the auditor still reports without 404(b)

"Unchanged" was wrong. In an integrated audit all deficiencies go to management in writing (AS 2201.81). In a financial-statement audit only significant deficiencies and material weaknesses must; lesser ones are at the auditor's discretion (AS 1305.04, .07). v2 also adds the SOX 302 certification row, which is unchanged.

§7.2 table · D-27, A2-24
Not supported

"The SEC's strongest argument"

The SEC explicitly chose not to index the $700m line (p. 42). It set $2bn to restore coverage of "nearly 95 percent" of float. v2 says that instead.

§1 · A1-16
Not supported

Dambra "supports the proposal"

He is supportive of the five-year on-ramp but cautious about widening the exemption, and other letters quote him saying it "is not costless". The quote itself is kept, with its cut marked.

§6 · B-05b
Overstated

Banks and the FDIC rule

The $5bn threshold comes from the FDIC (effective January 1, 2026), not the release. The rule applies to the insured bank, not the holding company, so a holding company that drops 404(b) may still need an attestation for its bank. The SEC estimates 87 of 153 banking large accelerated filers would become non-accelerated.

§2, decision 3 · A1-50, D-30
Missing a caveat

"In scope for fiscal 2026"

True only if no final rule takes effect before the FY2026 10-K is filed. Under the proposed transition, status is reassessed as of the fiscal year-end before the effective date, and relief applies from the next filing.

Exhibit 8, decision 2 · A2-17
